## General concept

Competing Consumers increase throughput by placing multiple workers against the same message source, each processing a share of the backlog independently. <Cite ids={["hohpe-eip"]} />

The gain comes from horizontal parallelism, but it only works well when the work unit can be isolated and the system accepts local ordering rather than one global sequence. <Cite ids={["aws-lambda-sqs-scaling","azure-competing-consumers"]} />

## Quick checklist

### Strong fit

<Checklist items={[
  "Messages are independent from one another.",
  "Backlog must drain faster than one worker can manage alone.",
  "Concurrency, retry, and DLQ metrics exist."
]} />

### Warning signs

<Checklist tone="warning" items={[
  "The same entity is updated concurrently with no protection.",
  "Global ordering is still assumed.",
  "The team is scaling workers to hide poor payload design or inefficient processing."
]} />

<Callout title={"Design tip"} tone="warning">
  <p>Before increasing concurrency, verify that the unit of work fits isolated processing and that the queue is really the correct parallelization point. <Cite ids={["hohpe-eip","aws-lambda-sqs-scaling"]} /></p>
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- Assuming global ordering on a standard queue.
- Scaling concurrency without reviewing downstream database or API limits.
- Ignoring deduplication and idempotent processing.
