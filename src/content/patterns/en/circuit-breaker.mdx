## General concept

Circuit Breaker treats a remote dependency as something that must be observed and governed, not simply hammered with blind retries. <Cite ids={["nygard-release-it"]} />

By opening the circuit after a failure threshold, the service preserves local resources, protects the user from unbounded latency, and gives the dependency time to recover. <Cite ids={["aws-circuit-breaker","azure-circuit-breaker"]} />

## Quick checklist

### Strong fit

<Checklist items={[
  "There are remote dependencies with instability or latency spikes.",
  "Failing fast is better than holding connections indefinitely.",
  "There is a clear degraded response or fallback path."
]} />

### Warning signs

<Checklist tone="warning" items={[
  "The breaker was configured without telemetry or threshold review.",
  "The system still retries aggressively at every layer.",
  "There is no half-open strategy."
]} />

<Callout title={"Practical rule"} tone="warning">
  <p>Circuit Breaker works best together with short timeouts, bounded retries, and capacity isolation; by itself it does not save an unmanaged dependency. <Cite ids={["aws-circuit-breaker","nygard-release-it"]} /></p>
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- Opening the circuit on raw counts alone without a time window.
- Using a breaker without short timeouts and still pinning local resources.
- Skipping product review of degraded responses.
