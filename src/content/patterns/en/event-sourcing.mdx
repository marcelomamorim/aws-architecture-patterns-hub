## General concept

Event Sourcing replaces the question 'what is the current state?' with 'which facts happened to get here?', turning the history itself into the primary source of truth. <Cite ids={["fowler-event-sourcing"]} />

That creates rich auditability, time-travel debugging, and the ability to rebuild state or projections without relying only on today's materialized view. <Cite ids={["aws-event-sourcing","kleppmann-ddia"]} />

## Quick checklist

### Strong fit

<Checklist items={[
  "Auditability, time travel, and replay create real business value.",
  "The domain can be expressed through events without major ambiguity.",
  "There is willingness to invest in event evolution and snapshots."
]} />

### Warning signs

<Checklist tone="warning" items={[
  "Events are treated as technical logs instead of business facts.",
  "There is no versioning or backward-compatibility strategy.",
  "The team expects to serve simple queries directly from the event store."
]} />

<Callout title={"Important reminder"} tone="warning">
  <p>Snapshots help performance, but they do not replace the event store as the canonical record of what really happened. <Cite ids={["fowler-event-sourcing","aws-event-sourcing"]} /></p>
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- Persisting events that are too vague to reconstruct business meaning.
- Confusing Event Sourcing with simple technical auditing.
- Leaving snapshots and replay without an operational plan.
